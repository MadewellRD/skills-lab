# Marketing Growth Stage Contracts

One entry per desk in the suite: what it requires on input, what it owns on output, and where it hands the `growth_packet`. The orchestrator uses these contracts to route; each member desk uses its own entry as the acceptance boundary for "this stage is done."

## Default sequence

```text
audience-segmentation
  -> positioning-messaging
  -> competitive-positioning
  -> brand-creative-standards
  -> product-marketing-launch
  -> pricing-packaging-communication
  -> sales-enablement-collateral
  -> demand-generation
  -> content-strategy
  -> organic-search
  -> paid-acquisition
  -> lifecycle-email
  -> conversion-rate-optimization
  -> growth-experimentation
  -> partner-channel-marketing
  -> community-advocacy
  -> events-field-marketing
  -> attribution-measurement
  -> budget-pipeline-contribution
```

The chain is ordered by packet dependency, not by calendar. A request that starts mid-chain starts at the earliest desk whose inputs are already satisfied, and a live brand or press incident, a deliverability collapse, or a spend anomaly enters at the desk that owns the surface regardless of what sits upstream of it.

Two dependencies are load-bearing rather than conventional. Everything downstream of positioning assumes the differentiators carry their proof, because an unsubstantiated claim propagates into the launch narrative, the ad copy, the deck, and the partner listing faster than any correction travels. Everything in the paid, lifecycle, experimentation, attribution, and pipeline stages assumes a tracking plan and agreed funnel stage definitions, because every figure those stages produce is a ratio whose denominator somebody chose.

## Stage completion rule

Every desk emits: source facts with attribution and collection dates, decisions, its artifact set, the packet fields it updated, assumptions labeled where they were used, open questions, halt conditions, and next-stage readiness. Provenance travels with every number and every claim. Unmeasured stays unmeasured in the packet, and a claim inferred from a product page is recorded as inferred rather than as approved.

---

## marketing-growth-command-desk

- **Requires**: the user request, the target outcome, the go-to-market motion, the operating posture, the audience exposure the work reaches, and whatever connector access exists for web analytics, ad accounts, marketing automation, CRM, search console, CMS, and event or community platforms.
- **Owns**: request classification across surface, motion, posture, and exposure; stage path selection; packet initialization and carriage; adjudication of conflicts between what the messaging document says and what the live site, the ad account, or the CRM shows; the workflow-level record; and the cross-suite handoff decision.
- **Hands to**: the earliest member desk whose inputs are satisfied, then each successive stage until the target outcome is reached or a hard halt applies.

## audience-segmentation-desk

- **Requires**: the customer and opportunity records that show who actually buys and who actually renews, win and loss records, interview or research evidence where it exists, the product's usable market constraints, and any segmentation or ideal-customer definition already in use.
- **Owns**: the segment set defined by observable attributes rather than by adjectives, so a record can be classified from fields that exist; the ideal customer profile with the qualifying and disqualifying signals behind it; the buying committee per segment, covering who initiates, who evaluates, who blocks, who signs, and what each of them is measured on; the jobs and triggers that put a segment in market, distinguished from the attributes that merely describe it; account tiering and the rule that assigns a tier; segment sizing with the list, query, or market source it came from and an explicit statement where a segment is unsized; the observed performance per segment across win rate, conversion, deal size, and retention, which is the honest test of whether a segment is real; and the anti-segments the company is deliberately not selling to, recorded so that later stages do not quietly re-add them.
- **Hands to**: `positioning-messaging-desk`.

## positioning-messaging-desk

- **Requires**: the segments with their buying committees and triggers, the product capabilities as a product source states them, the competitive alternatives including doing nothing, customer language from interviews, reviews, or support transcripts, and any approved positioning or messaging document already in force.
- **Owns**: the positioning statement per segment, naming the category frame the buyer already shops in and what it costs to teach a new one; the differentiators with the proof behind each and an honest durability note where a competitor can copy it in a quarter; the message house with pillars, supporting points, and proof points, written in the buyer's language rather than the product team's; the value proposition tied to the outcome the buying committee is measured on rather than to the feature that delivers it; the claim register, where every externally usable sentence carries its type, its substantiation with a date, the function that must approve it, and the list of surfaces it already appears on; the anti-positioning stating what the company does not claim and who it is not for; the naming and terminology decisions that later stages must not drift from; and the messaging drift audit comparing the approved wording against what the live site, the current ads, and the current deck actually say.
- **Hands to**: `competitive-positioning-desk`.

## competitive-positioning-desk

- **Requires**: the positioning and claim register, win and loss records with the reasons buyers gave, competitor public material read at a stated date, pricing evidence where it is public, analyst or review-site coverage, and the deals where a named competitor was present.
- **Owns**: the competitive set including direct competitors, adjacent alternatives, the incumbent manual process, the in-house build, and doing nothing, each classified by how a buyer actually frames the choice; the evidenced pattern of where the company wins and loses, drawn from win and loss records rather than from internal belief; the battlecard per competitor with their public claim quoted and dated, the honest counter, the traps a buyer should be asked to consider, and the objections that follow when the competitor is in the room; the landmine list of things the company genuinely cannot do against this competitor, recorded so a seller is not surprised by it in a call; the comparison content decision, including which competitive claims are defensible to publish and which invite a challenge; competitive pricing posture with the date the pricing page was read; the displacement and switching-cost analysis for accounts already on the alternative; and the refresh trigger per battlecard, because the failure mode here is a card that reads current and describes a product from eighteen months ago.
- **Hands to**: `brand-creative-standards-desk`.

## brand-creative-standards-desk

- **Requires**: existing brand guidelines and their approval record, the live surfaces where the brand currently appears, the messaging and claim register, trademark and legal constraints where they exist, and the markets and languages in scope.
- **Owns**: the voice and tone standard expressed as decisions a writer can apply rather than as adjectives, including what the brand does not sound like; the visual system covering logo, lockup, clear space, palette, type, imagery, and motion, with the source that establishes each rule; the creative review path stating who reviews what before it goes live and what may ship without review; trademark, attribution, and co-branding rules, including how a partner logo may and may not be used; the required disclaimers and legal wording per claim type and per market; the accessibility standard applied to creative, covering contrast, caption, alt text, and readable type at real sizes; the localization decision per market, separating what is translated from what is transcreated and what is left in the source language on purpose; the consistency audit naming the live surfaces that drift from the standard, with the drift described rather than scored; and the rebrand rollout inventory where a rebrand is in flight, listing every surface that has to change on the same date.
- **Hands to**: `product-marketing-launch-desk`.

## product-marketing-launch-desk

- **Requires**: the product scope and its confirmed release state from a product source, the positioning and approved claims, the segments the launch is aimed at, the destinations the announcement will point at with their readiness, and the launch tiering rule the organization uses.
- **Owns**: the launch tier and the rule that set it, so a minor release does not consume a major launch budget and a material change does not ship silently; the launch narrative stating why the market should care, written to survive being repeated by someone who did not read the brief; the audience and channel plan with the owner, the asset, and the release time for each; the destination readiness map covering the pricing page, documentation, in-product surface, signup path, and support readiness, each with its state, because these are what the announcement will point at; the analyst and press plan with the embargo, who is briefed, and when the embargo lifts; the internal launch sequence stating who is briefed before the market is; the launch metrics with their measurement source defined before launch rather than reconstructed after; the beta and limited-availability communication where the release is not general; and the go and no-go criteria stated as conditions rather than as a date.
- **Hands to**: `pricing-packaging-communication-desk`.

## pricing-packaging-communication-desk

- **Requires**: the pricing and packaging decision from its owning source, the affected customer cohorts with the contract terms that bind them, the notice periods those contracts require, the segments and their price sensitivity evidence where it exists, and the approver for external pricing wording.
- **Owns**: the external explanation of the packaging, including what each tier is for, which buyer it is aimed at, and the upgrade trigger that moves someone between tiers; the pricing page content and the entitlement wording, checked against what the billing system actually enforces rather than against what the plan document describes; the change communication per affected cohort, covering the effective date, what changes for them specifically, the grandfathering position, and the migration path; the notice schedule derived from the shortest contractual window in the affected set; the objection and escalation set given to support, success, sales, and resellers before the notice lands; the discount and promotion communication policy, including what a rep may say about price without approval; the plan retirement and end-of-sale communication where a package is being withdrawn; and the explicit separation between what has been approved and what is still a proposal, because a price quoted in a draft is quoted back as a commitment.
- **Hands to**: `sales-enablement-collateral-desk`.

## sales-enablement-collateral-desk

- **Requires**: the approved claim register, the positioning and battlecards, the pricing and packaging communication, the segments with their buying committees, the sales stages and the conversations that happen in each, and the evidence of what selling teams currently use.
- **Owns**: the collateral set mapped to the moment it is used rather than to the format it takes, covering the first-call narrative, the deck, the one-pager per segment, the technical and security responses, the objection handling document, and the reference story set; the talk track and discovery questions that make the positioning usable in a live conversation; the value and return calculators with their assumptions exposed rather than hidden inside the formula, since a calculator that a buyer cannot audit gets discounted to zero; the claim traceability from every asset back to the claim register, so a retracted claim can be found in every deck it reached; the enablement plan covering how sellers learn the material and what establishes that they can use it; the adoption evidence naming which assets are actually used and which have never been opened; the localization and partner-facing variants; and the retirement of superseded collateral, because the strongest predictor of an old claim being repeated is that the old deck is still the easiest one to find.
- **Hands to**: `demand-generation-desk`.

## demand-generation-desk

- **Requires**: the segments and their triggers, the positioning and approved claims, the pipeline or revenue target the program is accountable for, the historical channel performance with its source, the budget available, and the follow-up capacity the selling team can actually commit.
- **Owns**: the campaign architecture stating the objective, the segment, the offer, the channels, and the sequence, with demand creation separated from demand capture because they are measured on different horizons and confusing them is how a brand budget gets cut for underperforming against a capture metric; the offer design covering what the audience is actually asked to do and whether gating is worth the volume it costs; the target set with the arithmetic that produced it, working from the pipeline requirement back through stage conversion rates to the response volume the program has to generate, with every rate in that chain carrying its source; the lead qualification and scoring model with the behaviors and attributes that move a record, and the honest statement of which of those have any evidence of predicting a real opportunity; the routing and follow-up commitment agreed with the selling team, including the timing commitment, because an unworked response is a spend loss that no channel report shows; the program calendar and the interaction between overlapping campaigns hitting the same audience; and the recycling path for records that were qualified once and did not buy.
- **Hands to**: `content-strategy-desk`.

## content-strategy-desk

- **Requires**: the segments and the questions each asks at each stage, the positioning and approved claims, the query and demand evidence from the search stage where it exists, the existing content inventory with measured performance, and the subject matter access that gives content substance.
- **Owns**: the content pillars and clusters tied to the questions buyers actually ask rather than to the topics the company enjoys writing about; the inventory audit classifying existing assets as performing, decaying, redundant, or wrong, with the measurement behind each classification; the editorial calendar with owners, dependencies, and the subject matter source each piece depends on, which is the dependency that actually determines whether a piece ships; the content brief standard covering the reader, the question, the evidence required, the claims permitted, and the distribution plan; the distribution and repurposing plan, since production without distribution is the most common way a content budget disappears; the refresh and decay policy naming the trigger that puts a published asset back in the queue; the gating decision per asset with the trade between reach and captured records made explicit; the original research and customer story program where the differentiation has to come from evidence the company owns; and the retirement decision for assets that are wrong, superseded, or attracting the wrong reader.
- **Hands to**: `organic-search-desk`.

## organic-search-desk

- **Requires**: search console or equivalent query data with its date range, a crawl or index state of the property, the content inventory, the site structure and its technical constraints, and the competitive results for the target queries.
- **Owns**: the query set with measured impressions, clicks, and position, separated by intent so that navigational, informational, and commercial queries are not optimized as one thing; the technical index state covering what is indexed, what is excluded, what is blocked, and why, taken from the crawl rather than from the sitemap's intention; canonical and duplication handling including parameter, pagination, and faceted variants; the cannibalization findings where several pages compete for one query, with the consolidation decision per cluster; the internal linking structure that establishes which page is the answer for a topic; the authority position measured from referring domains with the source they were counted from; the result-page composition for the target queries, including the surfaces that answer a query without sending a click and what that does to the achievable ceiling; the migration inventory and redirect map whenever URLs move, captured before anything moves because it cannot be reconstructed afterwards; and the honest separation between what is measured, what is modeled by a third-party tool, and what is inferred.
- **Hands to**: `paid-acquisition-desk`.

## paid-acquisition-desk

- **Requires**: the segments and the audiences that can actually be targeted, the approved creative and claims, the budget and the efficiency target, the account access showing current spend and delivery, and the conversion events the platforms can receive.
- **Owns**: the channel and buying-model selection tied to the objective rather than to habit; the audience build per channel, including any customer list uploaded and the consent basis that permits uploading it; the creative and message set with the rotation and the frequency each audience is actually seeing, since frequency is the variable most often left uncontrolled and most often responsible for a rising cost per acquisition; the bid, budget, and pacing plan with the reallocation rule; the measured cost per outcome with the account and date it was read; the reconciliation between platform-reported conversions and outcomes matched in the system of record, with the attribution window each platform applied stated alongside its number rather than silently added to the others; the incrementality position, naming which spend has holdout or geo evidence behind it and which is credited purely by platform report, with branded search and retargeting called out because they are the two places where reported efficiency is most likely to be measuring demand that already existed; the brand safety, placement, and exclusion controls in force; and the landing experience each ad points at, because the auction is not where most paid budget is lost.
- **Hands to**: `lifecycle-email-desk`.

## lifecycle-email-desk

- **Requires**: the audience records with their consent basis and provenance, the lifecycle stages and the triggers that move a record between them, the approved messaging, the sending infrastructure and its authentication state, and the deliverability history where it exists.
- **Owns**: the program set per lifecycle stage, covering welcome and onboarding, nurture, activation, expansion, reactivation, and win-back, each with the trigger that enrolls and the criterion that exits; the consent and provenance position per list, with any list whose provenance cannot be established treated as unmailable rather than as a risk to accept; the suppression architecture covering unsubscribes, do-not-contact, active deals, open escalations, recent sends, and cross-program collision; the global frequency cap across programs, which no single program can set for itself and every program will exceed if left alone; the sending identity and authentication position, including subdomain separation between transactional, lifecycle, and prospecting sends so that a cold program cannot damage the reputation of a receipt; the deliverability read from delivery, bounce, and complaint evidence rather than from open rates, whose inflation by privacy-protecting mail clients makes them unusable as a primary signal; list hygiene and re-permission for records that have not engaged; the preference centre and the granularity of choice it offers; and the send-time, subject, and content standards that survive rendering across the clients the audience actually uses.
- **Hands to**: `conversion-rate-optimization-desk`.

## conversion-rate-optimization-desk

- **Requires**: the funnel steps with measured volume and conversion, the analytics and behavioral evidence for the pages in scope, the traffic sources arriving at each page and their intent, the offer and the form as they exist now, and the stage definitions the funnel is counted against.
- **Owns**: the funnel model with each step's volume and conversion rate carrying the denominator it was computed over, because a conversion rate quoted without its denominator is the single most transferable wrong number in this domain; the leak diagnosis naming where the drop happens and what evidence points at the cause, separating a traffic quality problem from a page problem, which are treated identically and fixed differently; the friction inventory across form fields, required information, unnecessary steps, load and interaction cost, and mismatch between the promise in the ad and the first line of the page; the offer and call-to-action assessment, including whether the page asks for more than the visitor is ready to give at this stage; the message match audit from source to landing page; the trust and objection elements the page needs for the segment it serves; the mobile and assistive-technology path, since these are frequently measured as one blended rate that hides a broken flow; the prioritized test and fix backlog, separating changes that need a test from changes that are simply defects; and the explicit statement of which pages have too little traffic to test at all and must be decided on judgment.
- **Hands to**: `growth-experimentation-desk`.

## growth-experimentation-desk

- **Requires**: the candidate hypotheses with the evidence behind them, the baseline rates with the periods they were measured over, the available traffic or audience volume, the guardrail metrics the business will not trade, and the decision authority for shipping a result.
- **Owns**: the hypothesis stated as a change, an expected direction, and a mechanism, so a null result teaches something; the randomization unit chosen against how the effect actually propagates, including account-level or geographic assignment where visitor-level assignment would leak between arms; the primary metric with its denominator and the guardrail set that constrains it; the power calculation converting the smallest effect worth acting on into the traffic and duration it requires, with an explicit statement when the required duration exceeds the window the business will wait, because running it anyway produces an underpowered result that will be read as evidence; the run rules covering whole business cycles, the fixed read point, and the treatment of early looks, since stopping at the first favorable moment is the most common way this domain manufactures a false win; the validity checks that matter here, covering assignment imbalance, contamination between concurrent tests on the same surface or audience, novelty and primacy effects, and seasonality; the read with the effect and its interval rather than a bare percentage; the decision record covering ship, revert, iterate, or inconclusive with who decided; and the holdout position, including which long-running programs have never been measured against no treatment at all.
- **Hands to**: `partner-channel-marketing-desk`.

## partner-channel-marketing-desk

- **Requires**: the partner set with the agreements that govern joint marketing, the tiering rule, the co-marketing funds available and their claim process, the joint value proposition evidence, and the lead-sharing and registration mechanics that exist.
- **Owns**: the partner marketing plan per tier, distinguishing co-marketing, co-selling, reseller, referral, and marketplace listing motions because they need different assets and credit differently; the joint value proposition stating what the combination does that neither side does alone, which is the part most partner content omits; the co-branded asset set with logo and claim rights confirmed against the agreement rather than assumed from goodwill; the development fund plan with the claim process, the evidence each claim requires, and the spend to date; the marketplace and directory listing content, including the categories and search terms the listing is actually found under; the lead sharing, registration, and credit rules agreed in writing, because unregistered leads and disputed credit are what end partner programs; the partner enablement material, which is the same problem as internal enablement with less control over the messenger; the syndication rules stating what a partner may publish under their own name; and the measured contribution per partner with the source, separating what a partner sourced from what they were merely present for.
- **Hands to**: `community-advocacy-desk`.

## community-advocacy-desk

- **Requires**: the existing community surfaces both owned and unowned, the customer relationships that can support advocacy, the permission records that exist for using a customer's name, the review site presence, and the escalation ownership for public conversation.
- **Owns**: the community program design covering the forum, the reason a member returns, the moderation ownership, and the code of conduct, with the honest position on whether a community is warranted at all; the advocate and reference program with each customer's permission state, what the permission covers, when it expires, and how often that reference has already been used, because reference fatigue burns the relationship faster than any single ask; the case study and testimonial pipeline with the approval chain and the claim substantiation each story depends on; the review site position with volume, rating, and recency read from the site, and the solicitation approach kept inside the review platform's own rules; the user group, ambassador, and champion structure with what participants actually receive; the moderation and escalation path for negative public threads, including the threshold at which it stops being a marketing response; the social listening position stating what is actually monitored and what is not; and the contribution measurement that avoids crediting community for pipeline it merely touched.
- **Hands to**: `events-field-marketing-desk`.

## events-field-marketing-desk

- **Requires**: the target accounts and segments the event is meant to reach, the committed and available budget with what it covers, the event calendar and its commitment deadlines, the capture mechanics available at the venue, and the follow-up capacity of the team that will work the leads.
- **Owns**: the event portfolio decision across trade shows, owned events, field dinners, roadshows, webinars, sponsorships, and community meetups, with the audience each actually reaches rather than the audience the organizer's deck claims; the fully loaded cost per event including sponsorship, build, travel, and staff time, stating what is excluded, because the sponsorship line alone understates the real cost by a wide margin; the pre-event demand plan that determines attendance, since a booth without a pre-booked meeting schedule is a cost with a hope attached; the capture mechanism and what the attendee actually consented to at the moment of capture, which governs whether the list can be mailed at all; the on-site experience, staffing, demonstration, and qualifying conversation; the follow-up plan with named owners and a timing commitment, which is where most event value is lost; the measured result covering meetings, opportunities, and pipeline with the date the record was read and the lag still outstanding, since an event's real return is not knowable for a full sales cycle; and the renewal or drop decision for recurring commitments, made against evidence rather than against the fact that the slot was booked last year.
- **Hands to**: `attribution-measurement-desk`.

## attribution-measurement-desk

- **Requires**: the tracking implementation as it actually fires, the campaign parameter conventions in use, the platform reports with their attribution windows, the system-of-record opportunity data, the consent and blocking position, and the stage definitions the funnel is counted against.
- **Owns**: the tracking plan stating the events, parameters, and naming conventions that are actually enforced, and the enforcement point that rejects a non-conforming link; the identity resolution path from anonymous visit to known record to account, naming exactly where the join breaks, because that break is where most attribution disputes originate; the measurable share of activity, quantifying traffic lost to consent refusal, blocking, and dark channels rather than treating the measured subset as the whole; the attribution model position, stating which models are computed, where each is computed, and who reads which one as truth, with first touch, last touch, and multi-touch kept as separate readings rather than blended into a single number nobody can reproduce; the double-count map showing where the same outcome is claimed by several channel reports, with the platform windows that produce the overlap; the incrementality evidence from holdout, geographic, or media mix work, including the explicit list of spend that has none; self-reported attribution as a complement with its question wording and response rate; the reconciliation between platform-reported outcomes and the system of record with the variance stated rather than resolved; and the single statement of which number the organization treats as the reported one, so that four dashboards stop being four answers.
- **Hands to**: `budget-pipeline-contribution-desk`.

## budget-pipeline-contribution-desk

- **Requires**: the approved budget with its source, the committed and spent figures per program, the pipeline and revenue targets, the opportunity records with their stages and values, the sales cycle and stage conversion evidence, and the sourced and influenced definitions in force.
- **Owns**: the budget allocation across programs with the constraint made explicit, since this is a single decision over a fixed total rather than a set of independent program approvals; the pacing position comparing spend to date against plan with the date it was read, and the commitments that cannot be reallocated inside the period; the working and non-working split; the pipeline contribution report using the sourced and influenced definitions actually agreed with the selling team, with a disputed definition recorded as disputed rather than resolved toward whichever produces the better number; the coverage position comparing pipeline against target with the stage conversion and cycle length that make coverage meaningful; the acquisition cost and payback with an explicit statement of what sits inside the numerator, because a cost per acquisition that excludes headcount, tooling, or agency fees is not comparable to one that includes them; the lag position stating how much of the current period's spend cannot yet have produced a measurable outcome, which is the honest answer to a mid-quarter demand for return; the scenario view showing what a budget increase or cut does to pipeline given the measured conversion chain and the lag; and the reporting package for the executive audience, separating what is measured from what is modeled.
- **Hands to**: the orchestrator for workflow close, and back to `demand-generation-desk` or `attribution-measurement-desk` when the contribution analysis shows the target arithmetic or the measurement basis that has to change before the next period is planned.

## Cross-suite boundary

These hand outward rather than to another desk in this suite: product strategy, roadmap, requirements, and the pricing decision itself go to the Product suite, while this suite owns how price and packaging are explained; individual deal execution, rep-owned outbound, forecasting, and proposals go to the Sales suite, while this suite owns the collateral and the claims inside it; adoption programs, health scoring, and renewal plays go to the Customer Success suite, while this suite owns the advocacy assets those relationships make possible; site implementation, page performance, and tracking instrumentation engineering go to the Web Development suite, while this suite owns the intent and the measurement plan; warehouse modeling and metric semantics go to the Data suite; lawful basis, consent architecture, and subject requests go to the Privacy suite; trademark, partner agreements, and formal legal claim review go to the Legal suite; budget approval, purchase orders, and revenue recognition go to the Finance suite; employer brand and recruitment marketing go to the People suite. Label the handoff explicitly so nobody reads those desks as members of this one.
